# Facebook, campaigns and politics: research by Dr Tristan Hotham

Open-access research on how political parties, leaders and campaigns use Facebook and other platforms, and how audiences respond, in Britain, Europe and the United States. This repository is an index: every paper has a DOI, a free PDF and, where the terms of collection allow, aggregate data on Zenodo.

- **Website and abstracts:** https://tristanhotham.com/research/
- **Short answers drawn from the papers:** https://tristanhotham.com/research/questions/
- **ORCID:** https://orcid.org/0000-0003-4931-9349
- **Google Scholar:** https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=RN6xlggAAAAJ

To cite a paper, use its DOI or the entries in [`papers.bib`](papers.bib). To cite this body of work as a whole, use GitHub's **Cite this repository** button, which reads [`CITATION.cff`](CITATION.cff) and points to the PhD thesis.

Preprints are not peer reviewed. Post-level social media data are not shared, in line with the terms under which they were collected.

## Britain: parties, leaders and campaigns

- **[Who Pays for the Party? The Structure of British Party Advertising on Meta, 2019 to 2026](https://tristanhotham.com/research/who-pays-for-the-party/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23415022](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23415022).  
  Who actually pays for British party advertising on Facebook and Instagram, and through which pages? I use Meta's public Ad Library Report for the United Kingdom, which lists every advertiser running ads about social issues, elections or politics from October 2019 to October 2026: 90,565 pages that spent about £252 million on 1.51 million ads.

- **[Paid and Earned on Facebook: Do British Parties Pay for the Pages that People Engage With?](https://tristanhotham.com/research/paid-and-earned-on-facebook/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23415020](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23415020).  
  Parties and their leaders run Facebook pages that attract audiences without payment, and they also pay Meta to put ads in front of people. Do they pay for the pages that people already engage with?

- **[Who Carries the Party on Facebook? Leaders, Party Pages and the Polls in Britain, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/who-carries-the-party-on-facebook/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23263763](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23263763).  
  Is a British party's Facebook audience built around its leader or its party page, and does either track the polls?

- **[Follow the Leader? How British Parties Divided the Work between Leader Pages and Party Pages on Facebook at the 2015 and 2017 General Elections](https://tristanhotham.com/research/follow-the-leader/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23280204](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23280204).  
  British parties campaign on Facebook through two kinds of page: the party's own page and the page of its leader. Do the two do the same job?

- **[Same Message, Thousands of Times: Targeted Facebook Advertising by British Parties at the 2017 and 2019 General Elections](https://tristanhotham.com/research/same-message-thousands-of-times/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23264728](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23264728).  
  What did British parties say in their targeted Facebook ads, how often did they say it, and what did they spend?

- **[Who Gets to Be in the Picture? Political Identity Ownership on Labour and Conservative Facebook Pages at the 2015 and 2017 General Elections](https://tristanhotham.com/research/who-gets-to-be-in-the-picture/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23279344](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23279344).  
  Parties do not only tell voters what they will do. They also show voters who they are for. Kreiss, Lawrence and McGregor call this political identity ownership: the work a party does to be seen as the natural home of particular groups of people.

- **[What Does a Satellite Campaign Do? Momentum, the Labour Party and Jeremy Corbyn on Facebook in the 2017 General Election](https://tristanhotham.com/research/what-does-a-satellite-campaign-do/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23296711](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23296711).  
  Satellite campaigns, organisations that campaign for a party without being part of it, became a visible part of British elections in 2017. The best known was Momentum, formed to support Jeremy Corbyn's leadership of the Labour Party.

- **[From the Doorstep to the Debate: Momentum's Facebook Page in the 2017 General Election and the 2018 Permanent Campaign](https://tristanhotham.com/research/from-the-doorstep-to-the-debate/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23279941](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23279941).  
  Satellite campaigns, organisations that campaign for a party without being part of it, are now a familiar part of British elections. We know less about what they do between elections.

- **[After the Referendum: The SNP, Plaid Cymru and Facebook, 2014 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/after-the-referendum/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23301374](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23301374).  
  The 2014 Scottish independence referendum transformed the Scottish National Party. Its membership roughly quadrupled, and eight months later it won 56 of Scotland's 59 Westminster seats.

- **[A Pause in the Mockery: Government, Party and Politician Pages on Facebook in Britain's First Covid-19 Wave](https://tristanhotham.com/research/a-pause-in-the-mockery/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23297886](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23297886).  
  When a crisis strikes, does political attention turn from parties to government? I test this on Facebook in Britain using 274,047 posts from 437 political pages tracked by CrowdTangle between June 2018 and October 2020: government and Parliament pages, party pages and politicians' pages.

- **[Who Won the Facebook Referendum? Engagement, Tone and Nationalism on UK Party and Campaign Pages, February to June 2016](https://tristanhotham.com/research/who-won-the-facebook-referendum/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23260111](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23260111).  
  Between the announcement of the EU referendum on 20 February 2016 and polling day on 23 June 2016, the Leave and Remain sides fought a large part of their campaign on Facebook.

- **[Does Facebook Lead Public Attention or Follow It? Facebook Engagement, Wikipedia Pageviews and Google Searches for British Parties and Leaders, 2015 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/does-facebook-lead-public-attention/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23399783](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23399783).  
  Engagement on politicians' Facebook pages is often read as a sign of public interest. I ask how closely it tracks two free measures of public attention, Wikipedia pageviews and Google searches, and whether it moves before or after them.

- **[What Earns Engagement? Hand-Coded Content and Facebook Interactions on British Party and Leader Pages, 2015 and 2017](https://tristanhotham.com/research/what-earns-engagement/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23398393](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23398393).  
  Which kinds of campaign post draw the most likes, comments and shares? I link a hand-coded dataset of 1,205 Facebook posts from the Labour and Conservative party pages and the pages of David Cameron, Ed Miliband, Theresa May and Jeremy Corbyn, published in the month before the 2015 and 2017 UK general elections, to the interactions each post received.


## Britain: comment sections and the far right

- **[What Went Viral on Britain First's Facebook Page? Outrage, Questions and the Cost of Mobilisation, 2013–2018](https://tristanhotham.com/research/what-went-viral-on-britain-firsts-facebook-page/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23257705](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23257705).  
  Britain First built a large following on Facebook before the platform removed its pages on 14 March 2018. The page and its post-level record are no longer publicly available.

- **[New Leader, New Crowd: Labour's Facebook Comment Section from Miliband to Corbyn](https://tristanhotham.com/research/new-leader-new-crowd/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23298975](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23298975).  
  Jeremy Corbyn's election as Labour leader in September 2015 brought a surge of new members and a reputation for an unusually energetic, and unusually combative, online following.

- **[One City, Two Comment Sections: Toxicity and Identity on Sadiq Khan's and Zac Goldsmith's Facebook Pages, 2016](https://tristanhotham.com/research/one-city-two-comment-sections/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23288551](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23288551).  
  The 2016 London mayoral election was contested by Sadiq Khan, who became the city's first Muslim mayor, and Zac Goldsmith. Religion and extremism became themes of the campaign.


## Europe: populism and new parties

- **[Do Populists Win on Facebook? The Engagement Premium of Populist Parties in Europe, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/do-populists-win-on-facebook/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23270349](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23270349).  
  Do populist parties get more attention on Facebook than other parties, and is that true everywhere? I use 669,760 posts from 112 official party pages in 17 European countries, posted between January 2010 and September 2020, with 186,939 posts from 74 party leaders as a second sample.

- **[Not a Forecast: The Radical Right on Facebook in Sweden, Norway and Denmark, 2014 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/not-a-forecast/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23302596](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23302596).  
  Radical-right parties often draw more engagement on Facebook than their share of the vote would suggest. Does that advantage tell us anything about where their support is heading?

- **[Launched the Same Week: Podemos, Vox and Facebook in Spain, 2014 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/launched-the-same-week/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23302075](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23302075).  
  Podemos and Vox were both presented to the press in Madrid in the same week of January 2014. Podemos won 8% of the vote at the European election four months later and 21% with its regional allies at the general election of December 2015.

- **[Ahead of the Polls? Sinn Féin, Facebook and the 2020 Irish General Election](https://tristanhotham.com/research/ahead-of-the-polls/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23300887](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23300887).  
  Sinn Féin's first place in the Irish general election of February 2020 surprised most observers. The party had lost half its council seats in May 2019 and polled at about 14% in October and November. I ask whether Facebook saw the surge coming.


## Europe-wide: 2010 to 2020 studies

- **[Did Brexit Travel? Eurosceptic Parties across Europe on Facebook after the 2016 Referendum](https://tristanhotham.com/research/did-brexit-travel/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23418607](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23418607).  
  Eurosceptic parties across Europe might be expected to have drawn attention from the United Kingdom's vote to leave the European Union on 23 June 2016. I test whether engagement with the Facebook pages of Eurosceptic parties and leaders in 19 other European countries changed around the referendum and later Brexit milestones, using CrowdTangle data.

- **[Count the Interactions, Not the Score: What CrowdTangle's Overperforming Score Measures in Archived Facebook Data](https://tristanhotham.com/research/count-the-interactions-not-the-score/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23418605](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23418605).  
  CrowdTangle closed in August 2024, but many archived exports of Facebook posts remain in use, and each row carries an "Overperforming Score" that compares a post's interactions with an expected value. I examine what this score measures in 856,699 posts from 186 party and leader pages in 20 European countries, 2010 to 2020, exported in October 2020.

- **[Who Keeps the Crowd? Party Splits and Facebook Engagement in Europe, 2011 to 2019](https://tristanhotham.com/research/who-keeps-the-crowd/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23418603](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23418603).  
  When a faction or a prominent figure leaves a party to found a new one, does the party's online audience follow the breakaway or stay with the parent? Screening splits in 20 European countries in CrowdTangle data on 1,712 Facebook pages, I found ten, in seven countries from 2011 to 2019, in which the parent's page and the breakaway's pages (new party or departing figure) are observed around the split.

- **[Less Is More? Post Length and Engagement on the Facebook Pages of European Parties and Leaders, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/less-is-more/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23418597](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23418597).  
  Do short posts draw more engagement on social media than long ones? I examine this using 856,220 Facebook posts by 112 party and 74 party leader pages in 20 European countries, 2010 to 2020, collected through CrowdTangle.

- **[Ahead of the Crisis? Radical Right Parties on Facebook and the 2015 Refugee Crisis in Europe](https://tristanhotham.com/research/ahead-of-the-crisis/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23418584](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23418584).  
  Did the 2015 European refugee crisis show up on radical right parties' Facebook pages before it showed up in opinion polls? I compare 15 populist radical right party and leader pages in nine European countries with 68 other party and leader pages in the same countries, month by month from July 2014 to June 2016, using posts collected through CrowdTangle.

- **[A Dip, Not a Decline: Engagement per Follower on the Facebook Pages of European Parties and Leaders, 2016 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/a-dip-not-a-decline/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23416926](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23416926).  
  A common claim is that Facebook steadily reduced the organic reach of pages. CrowdTangle does not record reach, but it does record the number of page likes at the time of each post, which allows engagement to be measured per follower.

- **[Where Parties Send People: The Destinations of Links Posted by European Parties and Leaders on Facebook, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/where-parties-send-people/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23416928](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23416928).  
  A party that posts a link on Facebook chooses where to send its followers. I classify the destinations of 410,308 link posts made by 112 party pages and 74 party leaders' pages in 20 European countries from 2010 to 2020 by the domain of each link.

- **[European or National? Common and National Swings in Facebook Engagement with European Parties and Leaders, 2013 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/european-or-national/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23416930](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23416930).  
  When engagement with a party's Facebook page rises or falls from one month to the next, is that part of a Europe-wide movement, a national one, a movement shared by parties of the same family across borders, or specific to the page? Using 14,510 page-months from 173 party and leader pages in 20 European countries, January 2013 to September 2020, collected through CrowdTangle, I split the variance of each page's deviations from its own trend into these four parts with nested fixed effects.

- **[Hits or the Long Tail? How Concentrated Is the Facebook Engagement of European Parties and Leaders, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/hits-or-the-long-tail/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23414620](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23414620).  
  Online attention is often described as a world of hits, in which a few items take most of the attention. Is the engagement that political parties receive on Facebook driven by a few viral posts, or spread across the many posts they make?

- **[Do Likes Predict Votes? Facebook Engagement and Election Results in Europe, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/do-likes-predict-votes/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23414618](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23414618).  
  Can the engagement parties receive on social media tell us how they will do at the polls? Studies of Twitter have reached mixed and often optimistic conclusions, but parties' own Facebook pages, where supporters respond to what parties post, have rarely been tested across many elections.

- **[Leader or Party? How Facebook Engagement Divided between European Party Leaders and Their Parties, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/leader-or-party/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23414616](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23414616).  
  The personalisation of politics suggests that leaders increasingly overshadow their parties, and social media might be expected to speed this up. I compare the Facebook engagement received by party leaders' pages with that received by their parties' pages, for 53 leader and party pairs from 44 parties in 11 European countries between 2010 and 2020, using posts collected through CrowdTangle and counting a leader's page only while that person led the party.

- **[Looking Them Up: Wikipedia Attention, Facebook Engagement and Votes for European Parties, 2015 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/looking-them-up/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23414630](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23414630).  
  Online attention to parties is often read as a sign of their support. I compare two kinds of attention to 112 European parties in 17 countries from July 2015 to September 2020: views of their Wikipedia articles, in the local language and in English, from the Wikimedia pageviews data, and interactions with their Facebook pages, from CrowdTangle.

- **[The Pivot to Video: How European Parties and Leaders Changed What They Posted on Facebook, and What It Got Them, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/the-pivot-to-video/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23414628](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23414628).  
  In the mid-2010s Facebook promoted native and live video, and news organisations famously "pivoted to video". Did political parties follow, and did it pay?

- **[Building an Audience: What Drove Growth in the Facebook Followings of European Parties and Leaders, 2016 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/building-an-audience/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23414624](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23414624).  
  A party's Facebook audience is the base on which its reach and engagement rest, yet little is known about how such audiences grow. I track the number of page likes of 112 party pages and 74 party leaders' pages in 20 European countries month by month from 2016 to 2020, using the counts recorded with each post collected through CrowdTangle.

- **[Second-Order Campaigns on Facebook? European Parliament and National Elections on European Party Pages, 2014 and 2019](https://tristanhotham.com/research/second-order-campaigns-on-facebook/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23414622](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23414622).  
  European Parliament elections are often described as second-order national elections, in which less is at stake and parties and voters invest less. Does this hold for parties' campaigns on social media and the response of their audiences?

- **[Posting in Office Hours? When European Parties Post on Facebook and When Their Posts Do Best, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/posting-in-office-hours/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23414610](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23414610).  
  When do political parties post on social media, and does the timing of a post matter for the engagement it receives? I convert the timestamps of 856,696 Facebook posts by 112 party pages and 74 party leaders' pages in 20 European countries, collected through CrowdTangle between 2010 and 2020, to local time.

- **[New Leader, New Audience? Party Leadership Changes and Facebook Engagement in Europe, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/new-leader-new-audience/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23414608](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23414608).  
  Parties often change leader in the hope of a fresh start, and in an age of personalised politics a new leader might be expected to bring new attention. Is a change of leader followed by a change in the engagement a party receives on social media?

- **[After the Votes Are Counted: Facebook Engagement with European Parties around National Elections, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/after-the-votes-are-counted/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23414604](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23414604).  
  Election campaigns are the busiest time for parties on social media, but little is known about what happens to their audiences once the votes are counted. I follow 95 party pages on Facebook through 229 party-elections in 16 European countries between 2010 and early 2020, using posts collected through CrowdTangle, comparing each page in the weeks after a national election with the weeks before the campaign.

- **[Equal on Facebook? Engagement and Vote Shares of European Parties, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/equal-on-facebook/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23414600](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23414600).  
  Does social media level the field between large and small parties, or do the parties that win votes also win attention online? Research on this question, framed as equalisation against normalisation, has mostly studied what parties do online rather than how audiences respond.

- **[Is There a Newness Premium? New Parties and Engagement on Facebook in Europe, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/is-there-a-newness-premium/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23414592](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23414592).  
  New parties are often said to do well on social media, which offers them an audience without the organisation and media access that established parties have. Do new parties' Facebook posts draw more engagement than those of established parties?

- **[Does Posting More Pay? Posting Volume and Engagement on the Facebook Pages of European Parties and Leaders, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/does-posting-more-pay/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23414560](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23414560).  
  Parties decide how often to post on social media, and a common worry is that posting more spreads their audience's attention more thinly. I use 856,699 posts from 112 party pages and 74 party leaders' pages in 20 European countries, posted between January 2010 and September 2020 and exported from CrowdTangle, to ask how the engagement a page receives changes when it posts more.

- **[Shared More, Liked Less? Facebook's 2018 News Feed Change and the Pages of European Parties and Leaders](https://tristanhotham.com/research/shared-more-liked-less/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23403682](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23403682).  
  On 11 January 2018 Facebook announced that its News Feed would favour "meaningful social interactions" between people and show less public content from pages, which it warned might see their reach fall. Internal documents later suggested that European parties felt the change pushed them towards more negative content.

- **[Love, Anger and Laughter: Do Women and Men Leading European Parties Get Different Reactions on Facebook?](https://tristanhotham.com/research/love-anger-and-laughter/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23403628](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23403628).  
  Women in politics receive more hostility online than men, at least in some settings and on some measures. Most of the evidence comes from the text of messages on Twitter.


## Europe: elections, governing and leaders

- **[The Party Page Carried the Message: Fidesz, Viktor Orbán and the Hungarian Opposition on Facebook, 2014 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/the-party-page-carried-the-message/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23419881](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23419881).  
  Fidesz won every Hungarian national election from 2010 to 2022, and from 2015 migration was central to its campaigns. I use 66,587 posts from the Facebook pages of seven Hungarian parties and two party leaders, Viktor Orbán and Ferenc Gyurcsány, from January 2014 to September 2020, collected through CrowdTangle, to ask three descriptive questions.

- **[More Votes Than Interactions: Romanian Parties and Politicians on Facebook, 2011 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/more-votes-than-interactions/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23418595](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23418595).  
  The Social Democratic Party (PSD) won 45% of the vote in Romania's parliamentary election of December 2016, more than the National Liberal Party (PNL) and the new Save Romania Union (USR) together. I use 36,922 posts from eight Romanian political pages, 2011 to September 2020, collected through CrowdTangle, to compare engagement on the three party pages with the vote.

- **[Small Parties, Big Pages: Engagement and the Vote among Finnish Parties on Facebook, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/small-parties-big-pages/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23418593](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23418593).  
  Finland's three largest established parties won about two-thirds of the vote cast for the parties studied here, but drew well under half of their Facebook engagement. I use 38,729 posts from eight Finnish party pages, January 2010 to September 2020, collected through CrowdTangle.

- **[The Quiet Centre: Portuguese Parties and Politicians on Facebook, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/the-quiet-centre/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23418588](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23418588).  
  Portugal's two main parties, the Socialists (PS) and the Social Democrats (PSD), won 70% of the vote among the parties studied here in 2019, but drew much less of the engagement with Portuguese party and politician pages on Facebook. I use 153,649 posts from 12 party pages and 63 politicians' pages, collected through CrowdTangle, and compare each party's share of interactions in the 30 days before the 2015 and 2019 national elections and the 2019 European election with its share of the vote.

- **[The Health Minister's Spring: Alain Berset, Covid-19 and Swiss Parties and Politicians on Facebook, 2015 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/the-health-ministers-spring/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23418586](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23418586).  
  Switzerland is governed by a collegial Federal Council drawn from the main parties, and some of its members run their own Facebook pages. I use 11,126 posts from eleven Swiss pages, those of the SP, FDP and SVP, five party presidents and three federal councillors, from May 2015 to September 2020, collected through CrowdTangle.

- **[Does Governing Cost Parties Attention on Facebook? Incumbency, Coalitions and Engagement in Europe, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/does-governing-cost-parties-attention/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23398391](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23398391).  
  Governing parties tend to lose votes, and junior coalition partners often lose more. Does governing also cost parties attention on social media?

- **[Engagement Is Not a Poll: Facebook and 31 European Elections, 2011 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/engagement-is-not-a-poll/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23396653](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23396653).  
  Can Facebook engagement tell us who will win an election? I compare parties' shares of interactions on their Facebook pages in the 30 days before 31 national elections in 15 European countries between 2011 and 2020 with their vote, their final polls and their previous result, for 172 party-elections.

- **[Does the Democracy Matter? Party Facebook Engagement in Six Smaller European Democracies, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/does-the-democracy-matter/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23400606](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23400606).  
  Does the state of a country's democracy show up in how people engage with its parties on Facebook? I use 184,317 posts from 35 official party pages in Finland, Lithuania, Portugal, Romania, Slovenia and Switzerland, posted between 2010 and October 2020, with 60.6 million interactions, collected through CrowdTangle.

- **[A Smaller Rally: Governing and Opposition Pages on Facebook in Europe's First Covid-19 Wave](https://tristanhotham.com/research/a-smaller-rally/)** (Preprint, 2026). DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23396939](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23396939).  
  Surveys found that trust in governments rose across Europe when the first Covid-19 lockdowns began. I ask whether attention on Facebook moved in the same direction: towards the pages of governing parties and heads of government and away from the opposition.
